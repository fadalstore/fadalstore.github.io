<h2 id="intro">Introduction: The Return of the Queen of Twists</h2>
<p>Freida McFadden, the author who gave us the iconic Millie Calloway in <em>The Housemaid</em>, has returned in 2026 with a character that makes Millie look like a saint. <strong>Dear Debbie</strong> is a psychological thriller that explores the dark side of creative generosity and the thin line between giving advice and playing God.</p>

<p>The novel follows <strong>Debbie Mullen</strong>, a stay-at-home housewife in a quiet town who writes a popular "Dear Abby" style advice column called <em>Dear Debbie</em>. She seems like the perfect wife and mother, but as the story progresses, we realize that Debbie's "good advice" comes with a very dark price.</p>

<h2 id="plot">The Plot: A Double Life</h2>
<p>Debbie Mullen's life is spiraling. She recently lost her job, and her domestic life is becoming increasingly claustrophobic. To cope, she focuses on her column, <em>Dear Debbie</em>, where she helps local residents solve their problems. But Debbie isn't just writing letters; she's observing her neighbors, judging them, and eventually, intervening in their lives.</p>

<p>The narrative shifts between Debbie's perspective and the lives of the people she "helps." As the tension mounts, it becomes clear that Debbie isn't just an observer. She is a <strong>gleefully sadistic antihero</strong> who believes she has the right to punish those she deems "bad."</p>

<h2 id="ending">Dear Debbie Ending Explained: The Ultimate Twist</h2>
<p>The ending of <em>Dear Debbie</em> is what everyone is talking about. Throughout the book, we are led to believe that Debbie is a victim of her circumstances — a woman pushed to the brink. However, the final act reveals the terrifying truth: **Debbie is a sociopath who has been orchestrating the chaos all along.**</p>

<h3>The Payback</h3>
<p>Debbie decides it's time for "payback" against those who have wronged her or failed to follow her advice. The twist is that Debbie hasn't just been giving advice; she has been actively sabotaging the lives of her readers to ensure they *need* her. The "masterpiece" of the ending is the realization that the advice columnist was the one creating the problems she was "solving."</p>

<h3>Is Not a Happy Ending</h3>
<p>Unlike many thrillers where the villain is caught, McFadden gives us a much darker resolution. Debbie survives, and she isn't just unpunished — she's empowered. The novel ends with Debbie looking for her next "project," proving that she is on a whole other level of unhinged compared to McFadden's previous characters.</p>

<h2 id="review">My Honest Review: 5/5 Stars</h2>
<p>If you loved <em>The Housemaid</em>, you will adore <em>Dear Debbie</em>. It captures the same fast-paced, addictive energy but adds a layer of dark humor and sadism that feels fresh. Debbie is a "nontraditional antihero for the ages" — you might not agree with her, but you won't be able to look away.</p>
